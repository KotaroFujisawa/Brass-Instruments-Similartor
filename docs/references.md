# References and their role in the model

## Instrument acoustics and geometry

- Murray Campbell, Joël Gilbert, and Arnold Myers (2021), *The Science of Brass Instruments*, Springer. Primary source for bore geometry, mouthpiece resonances, nonlinear propagation, and radiation approximations. The York trumpet profile is from Figure 7.52, printed p. 373. The Conn 8H bore and 5AL cup are reconstructed from Figures 4.22 and 4.32b, with Figure 4.46 used for an impedance comparison. Figures 7.45 and 7.46 identify and plot the Alexander C tuba, and Figure 6.9 supplies the flare used to complete the reconstructed BB♭ profile. [Publisher and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-55686-0)
- Miranda Jackson and Gary Scavone (2025), “A study of impedance of brass instruments and mouthpieces,” *Proceedings of Meetings on Acoustics* **58**, 035019. The Bach 5C geometry table is a digitization of the measured profile in Figure 2. It extracts contour coordinates from the published figure, converts them to metres, and sets the axial origin at the entrance plane. The source article is licensed under CC BY-NC 4.0. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1121/2.0002165) · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/)
- Vennture Mouthpieces, Inc. (2019), *VennCAD User Manual*, v2.0.3, p. 16. The reference-model example supplies dimensions and volumes for reconstructing the Yamaha BB-67C4. The reconstruction fits those measurements with a modeled throat length and a backbore outlet matched to the tuba receiver. [Manual](https://www.vennture.mp/downloads/Documentation/VennCADHelp_v2-0-3.pdf)
- Antoine Chaigne and Jean Kergomard (2016), *Acoustics of Musical Instruments*, Springer. Background for distributed mouthpiece and bore acoustics, including Section 7.3.4. [Publisher and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-3679-3)
- Neville H. Fletcher and Thomas D. Rossing (1998), *The Physics of Musical Instruments*, second edition, Springer. Background on lip-driven brass instruments and excitation mechanisms. [Publisher and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-21603-4)

## Lip motion

- Seiji Adachi and Masa-aki Sato (1996), “Trumpet sound simulation using a two-dimensional lip vibration model,” *The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* **99**, 1200–1209. Basis for axial and transverse lip-tip motion, lip contact, gap-flow inertia, and swept-volume flow. Higher-register forte presets in this repository extend the pressure and lip-frequency ranges explored in that paper. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.414601)
- Vincent Fréour, Christophe Vergez, Bruno Cochelin, and Keita Arimoto (2025), “Modelling the lips of an artificial trumpet player system,” *Proceedings of Meetings on Acoustics* **58**, 035027. The authors derive a reduced artificial-lip model with cubic stiffness from a von Kármán plate description. The conference abstract motivates the added cubic term in this repository. Its numerical coefficient and its combination with the Adachi–Sato model are implementation choices. [Conference program and abstract](https://ismra2025.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ISMRA-2025-Final-1.3.pdf)
- Ukyo Inomata, Naoto Wakatsuki, Tadashi Ebihara, and Yuka Maeda (2026), “Numerical Investigation of Blowing-Pressure Effects on Artificial Lip Vibration in Brass Instruments,” *Journal of Signal Processing* **30**(4), 119–122. Investigates how quasi-static deformation and geometric stiffness can affect artificial-lip eigenfrequencies under blowing pressure. Provides context for displacement-dependent stiffness. [DOI and article](https://doi.org/10.2299/jsp.30.119)

## Bore propagation, loss, and radiation

- Harold Berjamin, Bruno Lombard, Christophe Vergez, and Emmanuel Cottanceau (2017), “Time-domain numerical modeling of brass instruments including nonlinear wave propagation, viscothermal losses, and lips vibration,” *Acta Acustica united with Acustica* **103**(1), 117–131. Reference for counterpropagating nonlinear waves, diffusive wall losses, and time-domain feedback in the CPU model. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04247) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3813/AAA.919038)
- M. Jackson and Gary Scavone (2024), “Modeled and measured impedance of brass instruments, mouthpieces, and bells,” *Proceedings of Meetings on Acoustics* **54**, 035004. Uses Bessel-function thermoviscous expressions, with particular attention to narrow mouthpiece throats, and compares mouthpiece, bore, and radiation models with measurements. This motivates the local circular-duct closure and separate attention to the mouthpiece. [Author manuscript](https://caml.music.mcgill.ca/lib/exe/fetch.php?media=publications%3Ajackson_poma_2024.pdf)
- Filipe Soares, Bruno Cochelin, Vincent Fréour, Christophe Vergez, and Keita Arimoto (2025), “Nonlinear acoustics in brass instruments using 2D complex modal bases,” *Acta Acustica* **9**, 68. Demonstrates an axisymmetric reduced model including nonlinear propagation, viscothermal effects, and an exterior radiation field. The project uses finite volumes for the bore and a separate equivalent-sphere modal load. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/aacus/2025049)
- Leslie Greengard, Thomas Hagstrom, and Shidong Jiang (2013 preprint), “The solution of the scalar wave equation in the exterior of a sphere.” Reference for outgoing spherical wave solutions and their time-domain representation. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0643)
- Pauline Eveno, Jean-Pierre Dalmont, René Caussé, and Joël Gilbert (2012), “Wave Propagation and Radiation in a Horn: Comparisons Between Models and Measurements,” *Acta Acustica united with Acustica* **98**(1), 158–165. Compares plane and spherical propagation and radiation approximations with measured trumpet and trombone bells. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3813/AAA.918501)
- Fabrice Silva, Philippe Guillemain, Jean Kergomard, Bastien Mallaroni, and Andrew N. Norris (2009), “Approximation formulae for the acoustic radiation impedance of a cylindrical pipe,” *Journal of Sound and Vibration* **322**(1–2), 255–263. Source of the causal, unflanged-opening rational reflection approximation in Table II, with coefficients 0.167, 1.393, and 0.457. The author preprint uses a shortened title. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3625) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsv.2008.11.008)

## Geometry provenance

The JSON records in `data/` identify book pages, equations, digitization methods, inferred dimensions, and assumptions used in the earlier models. `sources.json` describes the initial trumpet reconstruction, `nonlinear_sources.json` the prescribed-wave extension, and `sources_bidirectional.json` the bidirectional CPU model.

For the trombone, the book's graph-derived profiles are supplemented by the manufacturer's nominal 0.547-inch Conn 8H bore and Denis Wick 5AL mouthpiece dimensions. The reconstructed mouthpiece is calibrated using the book's length, volume, and resonance constraints. [Conn 8H](https://connselmer.com/instruments/trombones/conn-symphony-tenor-trombone-in-bb-8h) · [Denis Wick trombone mouthpieces](https://www.deniswick.com/trombone)

For the BB♭ tuba, Alexander's Model 163 specifications supply the 20.5 mm valve bore and 450 mm bell diameter. The source profile is the Alexander C tuba in the Edinburgh University instrument collection, and the reconstruction additionally scales the centerline length for BB♭ and calibrates its resonance spacing. [Alexander Model 163](https://gebr-alexander.de/products/b-oder-c-tuba-gebr-alexander-163m-messing-poliert-weite-bauart)
